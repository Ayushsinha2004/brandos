## Full Post

I've watched 50+ AI projects fail. It was never the AI's fault.

(The pattern was the same every single time)

Here's the uncomfortable truth most AI vendors won't tell you:

The technology worked fine. The strategy was broken.

---

𝗧𝗵𝗲 𝟯 𝗿𝗲𝗮𝘀𝗼𝗻𝘀 𝗔𝗜 𝗽𝗿𝗼𝗷𝗲𝗰𝘁𝘀 𝗮𝗰𝘁𝘂𝗮𝗹𝗹𝘆 𝗳𝗮𝗶𝗹:

1️⃣ No clear business problem

Companies say "we need AI" the same way they said "we need a website" in 2005.

AI is a solution. Solutions need problems.

If you can't finish the sentence "AI will help us _____ which will save/make us $_____"...

You're not ready to implement AI.

---

2️⃣ Change management? What change management?

I once built a beautiful AI system for a client.

The technology was flawless. Response time under 2 seconds. 94% accuracy.

Six months later? Nobody used it.

Why? We forgot to involve the people who would actually use it.

Implementation is 20% technology. 80% psychology.

---

3️⃣ Starting with sophistication instead of simplicity

Founders see impressive AI demos and want that.

So they try to build it. Months pass. Nothing ships.

Meanwhile, their competitor used ChatGPT with a good prompt and got 80% of the results in a week.

The best AI implementation is often the simplest one that solves the problem.

---

𝗛𝗲𝗿𝗲'𝘀 𝘄𝗵𝗮𝘁 𝗮𝗰𝘁𝘂𝗮𝗹𝗹𝘆 𝘄𝗼𝗿𝗸𝘀:

→ Start with one process that's eating your time
→ Define what success looks like (specific numbers)
→ Build the simplest AI solution that achieves 80% of the outcome
→ Get it adopted by ONE person first
→ Then expand

Boring? Yes.

Effective? Extremely.

---

I've seen this pattern across 50+ implementations.

The companies that succeed don't have better AI.

They have clearer problems and simpler solutions.

The gap between AI promise and AI reality?

It's bridged by strategy, not sophistication.
